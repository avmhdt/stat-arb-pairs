# Multiple testing in pair selection (FR-30)

## The problem

A formation window of the cointegration strategy tests every pair of the 1,000 largest stocks that share a two-digit SIC code: about 20,000 Engle-Granger tests. At a raw 5% level, about 1,000 of them would reject even if no pair were cointegrated. Picking the 20 smallest p-values from that pile and calling them "cointegrated" is how a pairs backtest turns noise into a strategy.

## The family

The family is every candidate pair tested in the window: all pairs within the window's SIC2 groups among the eligible universe, with both legs priced through the whole formation window. Its size m is recorded per window (`fdr_m` in `pairs.parquet`). Nothing that depends on the p-values, such as the half-life filter or Johansen confirmation, shrinks the family: those run after the FDR decision, on the discoveries only (spec section 16).

Each pair is tested in both orderings (y on x and x on y) and the larger p-value is kept. That makes the decision independent of which leg is called y, and it is conservative.

## Why FDR, and why BH as primary

- **Raw p < 0.05** controls nothing at the family level: about 0.05 m false positives are expected.
- **Bonferroni** (p < 0.05 / m, about 2.5e-6 here) controls the chance of even one false pair. With 252 daily observations the Engle-Granger test has little power (below), so Bonferroni leaves almost nothing to trade.
- **Benjamini-Hochberg (BH)** controls the expected share of false pairs among those selected, the quantity a trader cares about, at q = 0.05 (D-05). It is valid for independent tests and under positive regression dependence.
- **Benjamini-Yekutieli (BY)** divides the level by c(m) = 1 + 1/2 + ... + 1/m (about 10.5 for m = 20,000) and is valid under any dependence. It is reported beside BH.

## Dependence: pairs share legs

A stock appears in up to a few hundred pairs of its group, so the tests are not independent: a stock whose price wanders unusually far moves the p-values of all its pairs together. Whether BH's positive-dependence condition holds for this structure is not established, so BH's guarantee is not formal here. BY's is. The table below reports both; a large gap between them flags windows where the BH discoveries rest on the dependence assumption. Each stock enters at most one traded pair, which limits how much one stock's behaviour drives the traded set.

## Power at 252 observations

The test's power, not the FDR level, is what limits discoveries. With n = 252 and an OU spread with a 10-day half-life (AR coefficient 0.933), the expected ADF statistic is about -sqrt(n (1 - b) / (1 + b)), roughly -3, against a 5% Engle-Granger critical value near -3.34. In a simulation with statsmodels' own `coint`, power is about 0.5 at a 10-day half-life and 0.96 at a 5-day one. Long-half-life pairs are mostly missed. This is a property of the test at this sample size, reported in `tests/validation/test_cointegration.py`.

## What the headline table shows

Raw p < 0.05 runs below the count expected under the null in most years, because the order-invariant rule (the larger of two p-values) is conservative. BH finds far fewer pairs than `top_k = 20` in most windows (`windows_bh_lt_5` counts the windows with fewer than five), and after the half-life filter and Johansen confirmation fewer still are traded. This is risk R-03 of the spec. It is reported as a finding: q is not loosened after seeing it (spec section 16).

## The table

`results/tables/fdr_by_window.csv` has one row per window: m, the raw count of p < 0.05, the count expected under the null (0.05 m), BH and BY discoveries, discoveries with an OU fit, and the number traded. `bh_expected_false_max` is q times the BH discoveries, the bound on expected false discoveries among them.
